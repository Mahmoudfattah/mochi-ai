# Mochi AI — Phase 1 Reference Research

**Research snapshot:** 21 October 2026  
**Working product:** Mochi AI — a voice-first, Egyptian-Arabic personal companion for focus, roadmaps, and time management.  
**Purpose:** Research report only. This document does not propose a product tech stack, write code, or design screens.

## Scope and evidence standard

This is a desk-research report based primarily on public product documentation, official help pages, and public source repositories. Consumer-product “first 10 seconds” descriptions are **documented-flow approximations**, not stopwatch-based usability-test findings. Where a company does not publish the exact first-run sequence, it is marked as an inference or not verified. “Active” refers to visible repository/product documentation activity, not an endorsement of quality or a guarantee of future maintenance.

Three evidence labels are used:

- **Verified:** directly supported by the linked documentation or repository.
- **Inference:** a UX interpretation of the documented behavior, not a measured result.
- **Uncertain / not verified:** public sources do not establish the claim reliably enough to depend on it.

## Executive findings

1. **Mochi’s most distinctive idea is the character-first interface, but voice startup is the main platform risk.** Browsers require microphone access to be granted, audible autoplay can be blocked, and `SpeechRecognition` is not a uniformly supported web capability. A blank/character-only home is feasible visually, but a first-run experience that offers absolutely no activation affordance may leave users unable to understand or recover from microphone permission, autoplay, or recognition failures. [MDN: `getUserMedia()`](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/MediaDevices/getUserMedia), [MDN: autoplay](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/Media/Guides/Autoplay), [MDN: `SpeechRecognition.lang`](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/SpeechRecognition/lang).

2. **Do not describe “hands-free” as “always listening on every device.”** A browser session can be hands-free *after the user has explicitly enabled a session*, but always-on wake-word detection, reliable listening while the tab is backgrounded or the phone is locked, and continuous recognition are different capabilities. Public web APIs do not guarantee the same behavior across browser/OS combinations. Mark background and long-running listening as uncertain until tested on a device matrix.

3. **Egyptian Arabic is a model/engine question, not just a locale attribute.** `ar-EG` is a valid language tag and is explicitly listed by Google Cloud Speech-to-Text, but that does not prove a browser-native recognizer will understand Egyptian dialect, code-switching, names, or product terminology well. Speech synthesis also depends on which voices are installed or exposed by the device. [Google Cloud Speech-to-Text language list](https://docs.cloud.google.com/speech-to-text/docs/speech-to-text-supported-languages), [MDN: speech synthesis language](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/SpeechSynthesisUtterance/lang), [MDN: speech synthesis voices](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/SpeechSynthesisUtterance/voice).

4. **Strong open-source references exist, but their licenses are not interchangeable.** AIRI is an active, TypeScript-led, MIT-licensed self-hosted companion project; Amica’s main code is largely MIT with separate asset licenses; Open-LLM-VTuber has split licensing between its backend and web frontend, and its frontend’s custom additional terms can matter for commercial hosting or redistribution. [AIRI](https://github.com/moeru-ai/airi), [Amica](https://github.com/semperai/amica), [Open-LLM-VTuber backend license](https://github.com/Open-LLM-VTuber/Open-LLM-VTuber/blob/main/LICENSE), [Open-LLM-VTuber web license](https://github.com/Open-LLM-VTuber/Open-LLM-VTuber-Web/blob/main/LICENSE).

5. **The best product patterns combine emotional feedback with useful progress, not guilt.** Finch turns self-care into pet care; Forest makes focused minutes visually tangible; Sunsama guides reflection and daily planning; ChatGPT Voice and Gemini Live demonstrate conversational turn-taking and interruption. Duolingo shows that mascot-led reminders can be memorable, but its guilt-oriented edge is also a warning for a calm companion intended to support focus. [Finch](https://finchcare.com/), [Forest](https://forestapp.cc/), [Sunsama daily planning](https://help.sunsama.com/docs/usage-guides/daily-planning/), [ChatGPT Voice](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/20001274-chatgpt-voice), [Gemini Live](https://support.google.com/gemini/answer/15274899), [Duolingo on reminders](https://blog.duolingo.com/hi-its-duo-the-ai-behind-the-meme/).

6. **For the character, expressive state changes matter more than visual complexity.** An idle/listening/thinking/speaking/error/reassuring state system could be implemented with multiple animation technologies. SVG/CSS is easy to inspect and own; Rive has MIT-licensed runtimes and is built for interactive animation; Lottie has a mature MIT web player for exported animation; Live2D brings appealing character rigging but also model/SDK licensing checks; three.js permits rich 3D at a larger rendering and performance budget. Test on modest phones rather than assuming any format is automatically lightweight. [MDN: animation performance](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/Performance/Guides/Animation_performance_and_frame_rate), [Rive runtime licensing](https://rive.app/docs/runtimes/getting-started), [Lottie Web](https://github.com/airbnb/lottie-web), [Live2D SDK license](https://github.com/Live2D/CubismWebSamples/blob/develop/LICENSE.md), [three.js license](https://github.com/mrdoob/three.js/blob/dev/LICENSE).

## 2. Patterns to adopt

1. **A visible, understandable conversation state machine.** The character should not just blink randomly. Each state should communicate what the system is doing: idle, permission-needed, listening, detecting speech, thinking, speaking, interrupted, paused/muted, offline/error and done. ChatGPT Voice exposes text with speech, and Gemini Live distinguishes mute, hold and end. [ChatGPT Voice](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/20001274-chatgpt-voice), [Gemini Live controls](https://support.google.com/gemini/answer/15274899).

2. **Interruptible speech and explicit pause/resume.** People revise thoughts and speak over an assistant. Gemini Live and ChatGPT Live document interruptions, and Gemini distinguishes hold/mute/end. Mochi should communicate when an interruption was understood instead of leaving the user to infer whether it heard them. [Gemini Live](https://support.google.com/gemini/answer/15274899), [ChatGPT Voice](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/20001274-chatgpt-voice).

3. **One quick daily check-in that produces a useful plan.** Sunsama’s planning ritual begins with reflection and builds a realistic task list; Focusmate starts and ends a session with stated goals and outcomes. Mochi’s voice conversation should quickly result in “the next useful action,” not a long generic chat. [Sunsama](https://help.sunsama.com/docs/usage-guides/daily-planning/), [Focusmate](https://support.focusmate.com/en/articles/9110188-getting-started).

4. **Progress that can be seen and felt.** Finch links self-care actions with pet growth/adventures; Forest makes focus time visible as a growing tree. Mochi’s character can respond to completed tasks, recovery from distraction, and milestones in a way that reinforces agency. [Finch](https://finchcare.com/), [Forest](https://forestapp.cc/).

5. **Graceful degraded modes.** Because browser recognition and autoplay are not guaranteed, the product should have a plan for “mic unavailable,” “permission denied,” “I couldn’t hear that,” “speech output was blocked,” and “connection lost.” A text input or manual restart can be a fallback without turning the main home screen into a dashboard. [MDN autoplay](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/Media/Guides/Autoplay), [MDN SpeechRecognition](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/SpeechRecognition/lang).

6. **Warmth without pressure.** Finch provides gentler companion framing; Duolingo demonstrates both the memorability and the potential annoyance of emotionally loaded reminders. Mochi should be pleased when the user makes progress but should not act sad, disappointed, abandoned or angry merely because the user misses a day. [Finch](https://finchcare.com/), [Duolingo reminder explanation](https://blog.duolingo.com/hi-its-duo-the-ai-behind-the-meme/).

7. **A clear consent boundary for the microphone.** Siri setup and Gemini Live both require activation/setup, and the browser itself mediates mic permissions. Users should be able to tell when the microphone is active, pause it, fully end a session, and understand whether audio is processed locally or sent to a service. [Apple Siri setup](https://support.apple.com/guide/iphone/turn-on-and-activate-siri-iph83aad8922/ios), [Gemini Live](https://support.google.com/gemini/answer/15274899), [MDN getUserMedia](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/MediaDevices/getUserMedia).

8. **Modular open-source boundaries.** AIRI, Amica and Open-LLM-VTuber illustrate separation between character, voice, provider/backend and desktop/web distribution, while also showing why license boundaries need to be written down. [AIRI](https://github.com/moeru-ai/airi), [Amica](https://github.com/semperai/amica), [Open-LLM-VTuber](https://github.com/Open-LLM-VTuber/Open-LLM-VTuber).

## 3. Patterns to avoid

1. **Hidden, permanent listening without a clear session boundary.** A mascot-only opening must not imply that the app is secretly listening. Make active microphone state unmistakable, offer pause/end, and explain what happens to audio. Browser permission availability is not the same as informed consent for indefinite recording.

2. **Assuming a browser can behave like Siri at OS level.** A website does not automatically gain a platform wake phrase, locked-screen listening, or guaranteed continuation after the tab is hidden. Treat these as separate, device-tested requirements; do not promise them across all PWA installs. [MDN media autoplay rules](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/Media/Guides/Autoplay), [MDN SpeechRecognition](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/SpeechRecognition/lang).

3. **Starting spoken audio without user activation and then treating silence as a bug.** Autoplay policies exist to prevent unwanted audible playback; the app needs a calm, recoverable path when speech output is blocked. [MDN autoplay](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/Media/Guides/Autoplay).

4. **Guilt as a retention mechanic.** Do not make Mochi look hurt, abandoned, furious, or disappointed when the user leaves, misses a session, or changes a deadline. Duolingo demonstrates the potency of mascot emotion but also the risk of overbearing reminders. [Duolingo’s own explanation of reminders](https://blog.duolingo.com/hi-its-duo-the-ai-behind-the-meme/).

5. **A character that animates intensely while the user needs focus.** Animation should have quiet/low-motion states and avoid unnecessary CPU/GPU work. The browser’s animation options all have performance costs depending on what is rendered. [MDN animation performance](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/Performance/Guides/Animation_performance_and_frame_rate).

6. **A voice-only system with no way to inspect or correct the result.** A transcription, succinct confirmation, undo/correction, or optional typed input can lower risk when Egyptian Arabic, proper nouns, deadlines or technical terms are misrecognized. ChatGPT’s documented transcript/text pairing illustrates the value of a visible record, though Mochi should avoid reproducing a full chat UI on its landing state. [ChatGPT Voice](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/20001274-chatgpt-voice).

7. **Anthropomorphic pressure or false emotional certainty.** Mochi may show “I’m not sure I caught that” as a product state, but should not claim to know the user’s true feelings from their voice or suggest that Mochi itself is emotionally harmed by disengagement. Emotional animation should be expressive but not manipulative.

8. **Treating an open repository as blanket permission to copy every asset.** Live2D samples, 3D character models, fonts, voices, model checkpoints, and media may be licensed separately. Habitica code and content also have different license terms; Open-LLM-VTuber’s frontend has custom additional conditions. [Live2D terms](https://github.com/Live2D/CubismWebSamples/blob/develop/LICENSE.md), [Habitica license](https://github.com/HabitRPG/habitica/blob/develop/LICENSE), [Open-LLM-VTuber web license](https://github.com/Open-LLM-VTuber/Open-LLM-VTuber-Web/blob/main/LICENSE).

## 4. Concrete UX principles for Mochi

These are research-derived proposals, not validated usability findings.

1. **Character-first, permission-honest.** Returning users may land on the character alone once permissions/session preference are established. First-time users still need a comprehensible activation/permission path. If the browser requires user action or permission recovery, prefer a temporary, low-visual-weight explanation over a silent failure. (Siri setup; browser mic permission guidance.)

2. **No hidden mic state.** Distinguish “not listening,” “mic permission requested,” “listening,” “paused/muted,” and “session ended” visually and accessibly. The character can carry most of the signal, but do not rely only on subtle animation or color.

3. **Make every state legible at a glance.** Listening can use attention/ear/eye motion; thinking can slow or still the body; speaking can coordinate mouth/voice; errors can be calm and specific; waiting can return to a low-energy idle pose. Include labels or optional captions where needed for accessibility.

4. **Ask for one thing at a time.** Start with a small check-in such as how the user is doing and what matters today; guide them from objective → next task → plausible time block. Avoid requiring a complete roadmap, full schedule and habits inventory before the user can start.

5. **Optimize for Egyptian Arabic and code-switching in real conditions.** Explicitly test Egyptian colloquial phrases, Arabic/English technical terms, people/project names, dates, numerals, noisy rooms, quiet speech, and interruptions. Treat the actual recognition engine’s `ar-EG` performance as an empirical test, not a promise derived from a locale tag.

6. **Turn plans into recoverable suggestions.** Show the effect of a missed task as a reschedulable item, not a character punishment. Before substantial rescheduling, summarize the proposed change and let the user correct or reject it.

7. **Make focus emotionally calm.** When a focus session starts, reduce motion and visual intensity; allow a minimal “quiet presence” mode. Use celebration sparingly at meaningful completion, not every few seconds. Respect operating-system reduced-motion settings.

8. **Provide a graceful alternative whenever voice fails.** Offer a short retry, a repeat of the last recognized text, or optional typing. Make recovery possible without forcing the user to navigate menus away from Mochi.

### Practical interpretation

- **Microphone access is not speech recognition.** `getUserMedia()` gives access to audio after permission; an STT engine must still turn speech into text. [MDN getUserMedia](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/MediaDevices/getUserMedia).
- **Speech recognition is not a wake word.** A recognizer transcribes speech; a wake-word engine decides whether a trigger phrase was spoken; VAD detects speech-like audio segments. These roles overlap operationally but are not interchangeable. [Browser VAD project](https://github.com/ricky0123/vad), [Porcupine Web docs](https://picovoice.ai/docs/quick-start/porcupine-web/).
- **A browser locale tag is not an accuracy guarantee.** Google Cloud lists `ar-EG`; that supports the feasibility of a cloud STT route but does not verify every vendor, browser or dialect. [Google Cloud list](https://docs.cloud.google.com/speech-to-text/docs/speech-to-text-supported-languages).
- **Audio output needs a recovery path.** Audible autoplay is subject to browser policy. Start/resume interaction may be necessary before Mochi speaks aloud. [MDN autoplay guide](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/Media/Guides/Autoplay).
- **Keep privacy visible.** The interface should indicate when mic capture is active and explain whether audio leaves the device. Do not claim “local processing” unless the actual chosen recognizer and voice pipeline run locally.

### Minimum real-device verification plan

Before committing to a hands-free promise, test current stable Chrome, Edge, Safari macOS, Safari iOS, Firefox desktop and Android browsers on fresh permission state and returning-user permission state. For mobile PWA targets, test: first run, permission denied, permission revoked, headphones/Bluetooth, noisy room, long pauses, interruption while Mochi speaks, app switching, device lock, screen wake, low battery mode, network drop, and restarting a conversation after a browser ends recognition. Record the exact device/OS/browser versions and the result; do not promote “works on my machine” to a compatibility promise.

## 6. Open questions for the founder

These are product decisions, not research questions that an external researcher can resolve on the founder’s behalf.

1. **What does hands-free mean for the first public release?** Is the requirement “no mic button after one-time opt-in while the app is foregrounded,” a wake phrase like “يا موچي,” continuous listening during an active session, or listening while the phone is locked? These are materially different levels of effort and privacy risk.

2. **What should happen on a completely fresh install?** Can the character briefly ask for an activation gesture/permission, or must even the first frame contain only Mochi? How should the app communicate a browser-blocked microphone without adding a permanent navbar/button?

3. **What is the privacy contract?** Are audio chunks sent to a cloud service? Are raw audio, transcripts, summaries or voice-derived signals stored? For how long? Can a user delete them and run without an account? Which processing must be local for trust/community reasons?

4. **Which language behavior is expected at launch?** Egyptian Arabic only, Egyptian Arabic with English technical nouns, Modern Standard Arabic fallback, or Arabic/English mixed conversation? How should the character speak numbers, dates, task titles and project names?

5. **What does Mochi’s personality allow?** Is humor welcome? How expressive can frustration/surprise be? Are negative emotions ever shown, or should “upset” be reserved for clear in-fiction contexts and never tied to missed tasks?

6. **How much should Mochi decide autonomously?** Can it reorder tasks or modify a roadmap automatically, or should all schedule changes be spoken as proposals and confirmed before saving?

7. **What is the minimum useful daily loop?** Morning check-in, project roadmap, focus timer, task capture, end-of-day reflection, or weekly planning? Which one must work exceptionally well before expanding?

8. **How should the project handle voice when it fails?** Is optional typing/captions acceptable as an escape hatch even if the opening screen remains character-only? Should voice be the default with manual fallback available only on error, or a fully supported parallel mode?

9. **Which open-source license matches the intended future?** Is the goal permissive MIT-style reuse, copyleft reciprocity, or a source-available project that restricts commercial hosted forks? Decide for code, artwork, character models, animation files, generated voices, training data, and documentation separately.

10. **What can contributors safely change?** Define ownership for voice providers, character rig/animations, STT/TTS adapters, planner logic, accessibility, translations and docs. A contributor should be able to improve one area without needing to understand every other part.

11. **What cost ceiling defines “free-tier friendly”?** Set a target per user/session for STT, TTS, LLM, hosting and storage, and decide which features remain usable when free quotas end or an API key is missing.

12. **How will success be measured without manipulative retention?** Candidate measures include time from opening to first useful task, percentage of successful Egyptian-Arabic recognition attempts, successful recovery after permission/audio errors, focus sessions completed, and proportion of users who feel in control. Avoid measuring success only as time spent chatting with Mochi.

## 7. Short summary in Egyptian Arabic

**الخلاصة:** فكرة Mochi قوية لأن الشخصية نفسها تبقى هي الواجهة، وتساعد المستخدم يحدد أولوياته ويركّز بدل ما يفضل يتنقل بين شاشات كتير. أقوى مراجع للتجربة دي هي **AIRI وAmica وOpen-LLM-VTuber** من ناحية الشخصية والصوت، و**Finch وForest** من ناحية ربط التقدم بإحساس بصري لطيف، و**Sunsama** من ناحية الـ check-in وتنظيم اليوم.

أنسب مبادئ للتجربة: خلي حالة الميكروفون واضحة، وخلّي Mochi يقدر يتوقف ويتقاطع مع كلام المستخدم، وابدأ بـ check-in صغير يطلع منه **next action** مفيدة. لو المستخدم فوّت يوم أو حصل خطأ في الصوت، الشخصية ما تلوموش؛ تساعده يرجع بسهولة. وقبل اعتماد أي مشروع أو avatar جاهز، راجع ترخيص الكود والـ models والـ voice assets كل واحد لوحده.
